- Western dominoes can be used similarly to cards. And for the purposes of this site's content all dominoes used as 'cards' are Western ones, Double 6 or 9 sets.

## By 'suit' via Equal End Matching

- As the number of possible different 'suits' increases (as compared to standard playing cards) it begins to make sense to start making matching by suit/type via Equal End Matching.
- Each non- double domino belongs to two 'suits'... a 2-5 domino belongs to the suit of 2s, or the suit of 5s. 
- Doubles belong to a single suit (a 2-2 is suit of 2s). 
- Sometimes, doubles also belong to a 'doubles' suit (thus a 2-2 is suit of 2s and doubles). 

### As a single card (by suit and rank)...

- When playing a domino as a single 'card' against other players single dominoes... both the suit and rank are important. 
	1. The suit is what determines which dominoes can be played.
	2. The rank is what determines which domino will ultimately win.
- Doubles belong to a single suit and in 'single' card type domino games rank highest in their suit (2-2, 2-6, 2-5, 2-4, 2-3, 2-1, 2-0).
- Each non- double domino belongs to two 'suits'... a 2-5 domino belongs to the suit of 2s, rank 5; or the suit of 5s, rank 2. 
- In a trick taking game a player leads a card and the other players mush follow that suit if possible. In games that the goal is to win tricks, the highest rank played in that suit takes the trick (ignoring trumps that is). If a 2-6 domino was lead and a 2-4 was played, the player that played the 2-6 would take that trick.

### As sets/melds....

- When playing dominoes as part of a set... really just the 'suit' is important.  The player ultimately deciding if a 2-5 domino is played as part of a set of 2s or a set of 5s.
> If a player had a 2-5, 2-3, 3-4, and 3-5 in hand, they could have:
>  <ol type="a">
>    <li>a pair of 2s (2-5, 2-3) and a pair of 3s (3-4, 3-5),  or</li>
>    <li>a pair of 3s (3-2, 3-4) and a pair of 5s (5-2, 5-3), or</li>
>    <li>a triplet of 3s (3-2, 3-4, 3-5).</li>
>  </ol>
- In set building/melding, 'doubles' many times are also their own suit (a 2-2 is suit of 2s and doubles). 
- Suits might be ranked also, suit of 6 being the highest and blanks/0 the lowest, if playing with doubles as suit it would be higher than 6s. Thus a set of 4-5s would rank higher than 4-2s.
- Dominoes that shares a suit/value may be played as a set or meld. A 2-4, 2-0, and 2-2 would be considered: a set of 3-2s (or a triplet of 2s).

## By Weight

- Alternatively, dominoes could be ranked by Weight rather than by suit (via Equal end matching). 
- By Weight a deck of dominoes is asymmetric. 

| Weight | # of dominoes | 
|---|---|
| 0 | 1 |
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 2 |
| 4 | 3 |
| 5 | 3 |
| 6 | 4 |
| 7 | 3 |
| 8 | 3 |
| 9 | 2 |
| 10 | 2 |
| 11 | 1 |
| 12 | 1 |
- Matching is many times contingent on a pair of dominoes (both their weights) adding up to 12. As such there are two matches that can only be paired together the 0-0 and 6-6, and the 0-1 and 5-6. 

## Asides

### In playing cards...

- Suit matching in actual playing cards is typically not used as a mechanism to match cards together. In playing cards, matching or melding via their ranks is more common.
- As the number of possible different suits increases it begins to make sense to start making 'suit' matches. If a deck with 8 different suit's and values 1-8 as ranks existed. It would be equally challenging to have a 'suit' pair as a rank pair.

### Chinese dominoes

Chinese domino sets are typically used to play card style games, rather than Layout ones. Chinese domino are longer and thicker than Western ones and get shuffled and put in a woodpile rather than a boneyard. A set contains 32 tiles, has no blanks, and has civil and military suits. The civil suit has duplicates of each tile. Interestingly, there is a deep tie between Chinese dominoes and 2d6 dice rolls, the 2d6 combinations are named and divided into suits the same.
